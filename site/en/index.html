A counterexample to Bu--Gu--Jaffe's expected mixed-state inverse sumset estimate

Haobo Ma, Wenlin Zhang

Paper

How much does combining two independent copies of a system increase uncertainty, and does little growth imply that the original system is close to a structured state? Haobo Ma and Wenlin Zhang show that one proposed quantitative answer for mixed quantum states is false, using a two-outcome probability distribution inside a seven-dimensional system. Their examples satisfy a weaker estimate and do not contradict the earlier pure-state theorem.

The motivation comes from inverse sumset theory: one asks what limited growth under addition reveals about the inputs. The manuscript connects Tao’s classical entropy work with Bu, Gu and Jaffe’s quantum extension. Mixed states allow probabilistic mixtures of states; when diagonal in a fixed measurement basis, they are ordinary probability distributions. Here convolution applies an invertible linear transformation to two systems’ basis labels, then discards one output. In the example, labels are residues modulo seven and the retained label is twice each input, added together. This transformation is admissible because the squared coefficients sum to one modulo seven.

Entropy measures uncertainty; diagonal states have ordinary Shannon entropy. The quantum-doubling constant is the exponential of the entropy gained under self-convolution. The comparison object, called the mean state, is not an ordinary average: it retains only Fourier-like coefficients of absolute value one, representing perfectly determined constraints, and discards the rest. Relative entropy measures discrepancy from that state. Bu, Gu and Jaffe expected an upper bound using the doubling constant, the mean state’s rank, the original state’s purity, and a factor determined by its largest imperfect coefficient. Rank counts supporting dimensions; for diagonal states, purity is the sum of squared probabilities. This specific mixed-state estimate is the target.

The example assigns equal probability to zero and one. Four equally likely input pairs produce labels zero, two, two and four, hence probabilities one quarter, one half and one quarter. With natural logarithms, input entropy is log 2 and output entropy is three halves of log 2, giving doubling constant √2. Every nonconstant Fourier coefficient has absolute value below one: the relevant coefficients average two distinct unit complex phases, while coefficients involving nonzero basis shifts vanish. The mean state is therefore uniform on all seven labels, although the input occupies only two. Its rank is seven, and the relative entropy is log(7/2).

The contradiction needs no decimal approximations. Even replacing the coefficient factor by any number between zero and one, the proposed bound’s denominator is at least log 2. At doubling constant √2, its right-hand side is consequently at most half of log 7. Yet log(7/2) is larger, precisely because seven exceeds four. All expressions are defined and the denominator is positive. For any positive number n of independent copies, relative entropy becomes n log(7/2), while the bound remains at most n times half of log 7. The actual coefficient factor remains cos²(π/7). For these same product states, the authors prove that the weaker estimate has positive denominator and holds strictly whenever its permitted doubling upper bound is at least the actual constant.

The authors also classify zero entropy growth for diagonal states at these parameters. For independent identically distributed residue vectors X and Y, conditioning on Y makes 2X+2Y an invertible rescaling and translation of X, preserving entropy. Removing conditioning cannot decrease entropy. Equality requires identical conditional output distributions, forcing invariance under translations generated by differences between support points. The input must consequently be uniform on a translate of an additive subgroup—an affine subspace here. Conversely, these uniform laws preserve entropy, agreeing with Tao’s classical zero-growth characterization. They correspond to the diagonal normalized stabilizer projections in the manuscript. The two-point example is not of this type and has strictly positive entropy growth.

The note credits the single-system construction to trureturing contributors and gives written proofs for every tensor power. It reports exact-arithmetic checks of the seven-dimensional construction and output entropies through eight copies: finite corroboration, not the all-copy proof. A Lean source is cited, but its proof contents are not supplied here, so no particular formal-verification coverage can be inferred. The significance is a classical obstruction to this specific quantum bound, without requiring quantum coherence. The note supplies neither a replacement universal mixed-state bound nor an equality classification beyond its diagonal setting; its validation of the weaker estimate concerns this family only.
