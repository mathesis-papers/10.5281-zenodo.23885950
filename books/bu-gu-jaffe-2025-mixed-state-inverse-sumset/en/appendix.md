# Appendix: zero growth and structure

This appendix explains the unnumbered diagonal-state discussion in manuscript §3, after Corollary 3. It identifies the equality boundary of entropy growth; it is separate from the counterexample to the quantitative bound.

Let $X,Y$ be independent copies of any probability distribution on $\mathbb F_7^n$, $n\ge1$, and write $Z=2X+2Y$. Once $Y=y$ is known, the map $X\mapsto2X+2y$ is invertible because $2$ has an inverse modulo seven. Relabeling outcomes does not change entropy. Therefore

$$
H(Z\mid Y)=H(X),\qquad H(Z)\ge H(Z\mid Y).
$$

The second inequality says that learning extra information cannot increase uncertainty on average. Equality holds exactly when $Z$ and $Y$ are independent, or equivalently when the conditional laws of $Z$ given $Y=y$ are identical for all support points $y$.

Fix a support point $a$. Comparing the conditional laws at $y$ and $a$, and dividing the output labels by two, shows that the law of $X$ is invariant under translation by $y-a$. Let $K$ be the additive subgroup generated by all these differences. Invariance under the generators gives invariance under their sums and inverses, hence under every element of $K$. Every support point belongs to $a+K$ by construction. Since $a$ has positive probability, invariance forces every point of $a+K$ to have the same positive probability. Thus the support is exactly this coset, and $X$ is uniform on it.

Conversely, if $X,Y$ are uniform on $a+K$, then $2K=K$. For each target in $4a+K$, choosing the first input leaves exactly one compatible second input in $a+K$. The output is consequently uniform on $4a+K$, which has the same size as $a+K$. Both entropies equal $\log|K|$. This includes $K=\{0\}$ (a deterministic outcome) and $K=\mathbb F_7^n$ (the fully uniform law).

An additive subgroup of $\mathbb F_7^n$ is a vector subspace, because multiplication by any field element is repeated addition. A translate $a+K$ is called an affine subspace. For one coordinate, its possible sizes are $1$ and $7$, so the two-point input has strict entropy growth. In all dimensions the equality description agrees with the classical zero-doubling characterization in Tao, Theorem 1.11(i), §5.

## The diagonal stabilizer interpretation

To connect this classical description with the source's quantum terminology, define

$$
K^\perp=\{p\in\mathbb F_7^n:p\cdot k=0\text{ for all }k\in K\},
$$

$$
Z^p|x\rangle=\omega^{p\cdot x}|x\rangle.
$$

The projection onto the span of the basis labels in $a+K$ is

$$
P_{a+K}=\frac1{|K^\perp|}\sum_{p\in K^\perp}\omega^{-p\cdot a}Z^p,
\qquad \rho_{a+K}=\frac{P_{a+K}}{|K|}.
$$

Indeed, its diagonal entry at $x$ is the average of the characters $\omega^{p\cdot(x-a)}$. If $x-a\in K$, every term is one. Otherwise a nontrivial character multiplies the sum by a phase different from one, forcing the sum to vanish. Thus the projection has ones on $a+K$, zeros elsewhere, and rank $|K|$.

These are the diagonal normalized minimal stabilizer projections: simultaneous eigenspace constraints for commuting clock operators specify an affine subspace, and normalizing the projection gives the uniform law there. Conversely, a diagonal minimal stabilizer-projection state has no shift coefficients in its Weyl expansion; its remaining clock constraints specify just such an affine subspace. This interpretation concerns the computationally diagonal class at $d=7$, $s=t=2$; it is not an equality classification for all quantum states.

## Difference instead of sum

For the same two-point witness, the discarded-coordinate law $-2I+2J$ has probabilities $1/2,1/4,1/4$ at $0,2,5$. Its entropy is also $\tfrac32\log2$, so the complementary quantum-difference constant in BGJ Remark 43 is $\sqrt2$. Replacing the doubling constant by that constant while keeping the same proposed right-hand side would leave the contradiction unchanged. This is an additional observation about the same witness, not a refutation of every difference inequality.
